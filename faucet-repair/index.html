When a faucet problem is an emergency

A drip can wait. A faucet handle that breaks off and leaves water running, a supply line that bursts under the sink or a shutoff valve that sprays when touched cannot. A fast, correct response limits the damage and saves the cabinet.

Emergency faucet repair in Vernon Hills starts with shutting off the water at the lowest point you can reach. The valves under a sink turn clockwise to close. If they are stuck or leaking, the main water valve is the next step.

What to do while you wait

  • Turn the shutoff valves under the sink clockwise, or close the main water valve.
  • Place a bucket under the leak and lay towels around it.
  • Empty the cabinet so wet items do not stay against wood.
  • Do not force a stuck handle, because the part behind it may be the only thing holding back the water.
Common repairs

Faucet and fixture problems we repair

Most fixtures fail in the part you cannot see. Here is what we find most often.

Dripping spout

Worn cartridge, washer or seat. The part inside the faucet is usually the cause.

Leak at the handle or base

O-rings and seals that have worn out.

Low flow

Mineral buildup in the aerator or cartridge. Hard water in some systems makes this more common.

Stuck or broken shutoff valve

Old stop valves often seize or leak when turned. Replacing them makes later work safer.

Tub or shower won't stop

A worn cartridge or diverter. A shower valve may need the wall opened for access.

Leaking supply line

A braided hose or connector under a sink can burst without warning.

Shutoff valves and supply lines

The small valves under a sink and behind a toilet are meant to be turned only rarely, so many are stiff or corroded when you need them. A valve that will not close leaves you using the main shutoff in a crisis. We replace old stops with quarter-turn valves that are easy to operate.

Braided supply lines last years but not forever. A line that is bulging, rusting at the fitting or older than ten years is worth replacing before it fails. If the leak you found is a drain connection and not a supply, our drain cleaning page explains the other common cause of wet cabinets.

Tubs, showers and outdoor spigots

A tub or shower that drips after you turn it off usually has a worn cartridge. Some valves have a removable cartridge that can be replaced from the front. Others need access from behind the wall, and we explain this before cutting anything.

Outdoor spigots can freeze in a Lake County winter, and a split pipe inside the wall may not show until spring when the water is turned on. Water inside the wall is covered on our burst pipe repair page, and hidden moisture is covered by leak detection.

Code and permits note

According to the village permit guide, altering supply or drain lines is plumbing work under the Illinois State Plumbing Code, while swapping a faucet is routine.

Illinois plumbers are licensed through IDPH, which offers a license lookup, so you can check anyone you hire.
