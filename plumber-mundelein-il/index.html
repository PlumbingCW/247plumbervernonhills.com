When a Mundelein plumbing problem cannot wait

Most calls from Mundelein start the same way. Water is somewhere it should not be, or hot water has stopped, and it is happening at an awkward hour. A basement drain that bubbles after a storm, a water heater leaking onto the floor and a pipe that has let go in a cold snap are the situations we hear about most.

Emergency plumbing in Mundelein starts with a phone call. We ask where the water is, what you can shut off and whether anyone is in danger, then we dispatch. Emergency dispatch is available around the clock, including nights, weekends and holidays.

Our two most requested services here are water heater replacement and drain cleaning, and both are covered on their own pages in detail.

Before we arrive: if you have a burst pipe or an active leak

These four steps limit the damage in the minutes before a plumber arrives. They apply to a burst pipe, a failed supply line and most active leaks.

1Shut off the main valve

Turn the main water valve clockwise. It is usually near the meter or where the line enters the basement.

2Kill power to wet areas

Switch off the breaker for any room with water near outlets, fixtures or the ceiling.

3Clear the area

Move belongings, rugs and electronics out of the water's path and keep children and pets away.

Around Mundelein: where our calls come from

Mundelein is a village in Lake County, with about 31,560 residents at the 2020 census and one ZIP code, 60060. These are the parts of town we cover most.

Downtown and the Metra corridor

Older streets near the center of the village and the North Central Service station often mean older drains. Emergency dispatch available around the clock covers the downtown grid.

Around Diamond Lake

Homes near the lake, a 153-acre glacial lake in the south of the village, are close to groundwater, so sump pumps and basement water come up often.

Maple Avenue and the west side

Neighborhoods off Route 176 call about drains, toilets and water heaters. We ask about the age of the house and the pipe material when we arrive.

Local note

Storm season and the 2017 flood

In July 2017, about six inches of rain fell on the Mundelein area in seven hours. Village leaders called it a 200-year rain event, and downtown and many basements flooded. In 2019 the mayor described a stormwater plan of roughly $10 million that included new pipes and a detention pond on Courtland Street, as reported by the Daily Herald.

A sump pump and a working backup are the first line of defense, which our sump pump page explains. A sewer backup is a different problem, covered on our sewer line cleaning page. Lake County Public Works serves portions of Mundelein as well as Vernon Hills, so ask which system serves your address.
