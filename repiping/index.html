Signs your pipes need replacement

A single leak does not mean the house needs new pipes. Repeated leaks, discolored water, low pressure and visible corrosion usually do. Galvanized steel, which rusts from the inside, and certain older plastic and copper systems are the common candidates.

A plumber can look at pipe material, the pattern of failures and the water quality to tell you whether patching or replacement makes more sense. We explain both honestly, including when repair is the cheaper path for now.

Warning signs

What we look for

These are the signs that push us to recommend a larger fix.

Brown or rusty water

Corroded steel pipe flakes into the water, especially after it sits overnight.

Low pressure at several fixtures

Mineral and rust buildup narrows the inside of old pipe.

Repeated pinhole leaks

Thin spots in copper or steel fail one after another.

Visible corrosion or green stains

Fittings and joints show wear before the pipe fails.

Noisy pipes and water hammer

Loose or failing sections vibrate and bang.

Whole-house or partial repiping

A whole-house job replaces the supply pipes to every fixture. A partial job replaces a failing run, such as the kitchen and bath on one side, and leaves sound pipe alone. Partial work is often practical when only one area has failed.

The work is planned fixture by fixture. We map the routes, open small access holes and patch them, and we schedule water shutoffs for set periods so you are not without water for days. Most homes can stay occupied. Details depend on the house, and we explain them before work starts.

Permits and inspections

Repiping needs a permit in many jurisdictions. The Village of Vernon Hills publishes a permit guide, and its building department can be reached at 847-367-3700. Inspections are on weekdays, so we plan the schedule around them.

Plumbers are licensed through IDPH, which also provides a plumbing license verification lookup. Ask any contractor for proof of license before work starts. Our plumbing installation page covers new fixtures and additions, and our burst pipe repair page covers the emergency side.

What it costs

What repiping costs

Repiping is a large job, and price depends on the size of the home, the material and the access. We give a clear price before work starts, in writing, after looking at the house.

Insurance usually does not cover repiping for age or corrosion, but it may cover damage from a sudden burst. Keep records of leaks.
