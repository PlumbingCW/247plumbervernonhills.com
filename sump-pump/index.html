When a sump pump failure is an emergency

A sump pump sits in a pit and moves groundwater away from the foundation. When it fails during heavy rain, water rises in the pit and then across the floor. A basement can take on water quickly, and finished spaces are the first casualties.

Emergency sump pump repair in Vernon Hills starts with a few checks you can do on the phone: is it plugged in, is the breaker on, is the float free and is the discharge line frozen or clogged. If it needs a new pump, we can usually replace it the same visit.

What to do while you wait

  • Check that the pump is plugged in and the GFCI outlet has not tripped.
  • Lift the float gently to see if the pump starts. Do not reach into the pit with power on.
  • Move boxes, electronics and furniture off the floor.
  • Stay out of a flooded basement if water is near the electrical panel or outlets.
Failure causes

Why sump pumps fail

Most failures happen during the storm you need the pump for. These are the causes we find most often.

Power outage

Storms that bring the water also knock out the power. A battery backup keeps the pump running.

Stuck float

Debris or the pit wall can jam the float so the pump never turns on.

Burned-out motor

Pumps wear out. Many run for years but not forever.

Clogged or frozen discharge

A blocked pipe leaves the pump running with nowhere to send the water.

Pit too small or pump undersized

A pump that cannot keep up with heavy rain may run constantly.

Where the water is allowed to go

Lake County Public Works, which serves Vernon Hills, has a sewer use ordinance that bars sump pumps, downspouts, footing drains and window well drains from the sanitary sewer. Sump discharge belongs outside, away from the foundation, where it can flow to the yard or a storm system.

If your pump discharges to a floor drain or a sanitary line, the connection may violate that ordinance and can overload the system in a storm. We explain what is allowed and reroute the discharge when needed. Ejector pumps, which lift wastewater from a basement bathroom or laundry, are different and connect to the sanitary sewer. We install and service both.

Storms and flooding in this area

Heavy rain is the main threat. In July 2017, a storm brought about six inches of rain in seven hours to the Mundelein area, flooding many homes, and local news later reported a stormwater plan of about $10 million. A sump pump system is a first line of defense, though it does not stop sewer backups.

A sewer backup is a different problem, which our sewer line cleaning page describes. A damp basement that does not flood may be a seepage problem handled by basement waterproofing. We can advise which problem you have. Our Mundelein page covers service there.
