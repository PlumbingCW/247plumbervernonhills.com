When a toilet problem is an emergency

A toilet that keeps rising after a flush, one that leaks at the base or one that floods the bathroom cannot wait. Water on the floor damages subfloor, drywall and the ceiling below, and if the water is dirty it is a health problem.

Emergency toilet repair in Vernon Hills starts with stopping the water. If the bowl is rising, close the shutoff valve on the wall or floor behind the toilet. If it will not close, shut the main water valve and call us.

What to do while you wait

  • Close the supply valve behind the toilet by turning it clockwise.
  • Do not flush again. Each flush adds water to a blocked drain.
  • Remove the tank lid and lift the float or flapper to stop the tank filling.
  • Mop standing water quickly and keep children and pets out of the area.
Symptoms

What the toilet is telling you

Toilets fail in recognizable ways. These symptoms point to specific causes.

Water rises toward the rim

A clog in the trap or the line below. A plunger with a tight seal clears many of these.

Water at the base

A failed wax ring or loose closet bolts. The seal between the toilet and the flange is no longer holding.

Constant running

A worn flapper, a misadjusted fill valve or a chain that is too tight. It wastes water and raises the bill.

Weak or slow flush

Mineral buildup in the rim jets or siphon, or a low water level in the tank.

Gurgling or other fixtures backing up

A main line problem, not just the toilet. See our drain and sewer pages.

Clogs: toilet or main line?

If only one toilet clogs and other fixtures drain normally, the blockage is likely in the toilet trap or the short pipe below it. A plunger or a closet auger usually clears it. If a tub or sink drains slowly at the same time, or a basement floor drain bubbles after you flush, the main line is the likely problem.

A toilet that clogs again and again after clearing may have a venting problem, a partial blockage deeper in the line or a toilet that is not siphoning properly. Our drain cleaning page covers the line side, and our sewer line cleaning page explains the main line side. Items like wipes, feminine products and toys cause most stoppages.

Replacement: what we install

A replacement is often a same-visit job. We remove the old toilet, check the flange and the floor, install a new wax ring or seal, set the toilet level and test for leaks. Standard height, comfort height and compact models are all common.

Water use matters. Toilets sold today use far less water per flush than older models, which can lower your bill. We help you choose a size that fits your bathroom, and we haul the old unit away. If the flange or the floor is damaged, we tell you before work and price the repair clearly.

Code and permits note

Most toilet repairs need no permit. A replacement or a change to drain piping is plumbing work that falls under the Illinois State Plumbing Code cited in the village permit guide.

Illinois plumbers are licensed through IDPH, which offers a license lookup, so you can check anyone you hire.
