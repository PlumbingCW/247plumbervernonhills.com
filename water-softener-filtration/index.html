Start with your water, not a sales pitch

Vernon Hills water comes from Lake Michigan through the Lake County Public Works system. The utility publishes a water quality report that lists chlorine, fluoride and lead results, but we have no hardness number we would rely on, so the right move is to test your own tap.

A simple test tells you hardness, chlorine and sometimes iron. Many homes need nothing. Some want a softener for scale and spots, a carbon filter for taste or a reverse osmosis unit for drinking water. We explain what each does and does not do.

What it fixes

Problems treatment can help with

Treatment fixes specific problems. These are the symptoms people call about.

White scale on fixtures and kettles

Hardness minerals leave a crust. A softener reduces it.

Spots on dishes and glass

Dissolved minerals dry on the surface.

Chlorine taste or smell

A carbon filter removes much of it.

Dry skin and soap that does not lather

Hard water makes soap less effective.

Short appliance life from scale

Scale in a water heater lowers efficiency.

Installing and servicing systems

A softener connects to the main water line, a drain and an outlet. We install a bypass so you can isolate the unit, and we set the hardness and regeneration schedule. Filters go where the water line enters or at a single tap.

Service includes valve repair, resin and media replacement, brine tank cleaning and control head repair. We also replace failed units. If a system leaks at a fitting, the issue may be a connection and the repair is quick.

Water heaters and scale

Hardness minerals collect in water heaters, especially tankless units, which need descaling. A softener reduces this, and flushing helps either way. See our tankless water heater page for descaling and our water heater repair page for tank issues.

If your water is discolored, rusty or low in pressure, the problem may be the pipes and not the supply. Our repiping page explains that case. A treatment system cannot repair corroded pipes.

Code and permits note

Follow the maker's installation guidelines. A softener or filter ties into your supply and drain lines, so the connections follow the Illinois State Plumbing Code that the village cites. The utility's water quality report is the place to start, and it does not give a hardness figure we would rely on.
