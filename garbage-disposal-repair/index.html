When a garbage disposal problem is an emergency

A disposal that hums and will not turn is a minor problem. One that leaks onto the cabinet floor, or sends dirty water back up the other side of a double sink, needs attention now. Water in a cabinet damages wood and can spread to the floor.

Emergency garbage disposal repair in Vernon Hills starts with safety. Turn off the switch and the breaker if you can, and never reach into the grinding chamber. Most jams are cleared with a hex key from underneath and a few minutes of work.

What to do while you wait

  • Switch the disposal off at the wall and, if possible, at the breaker.
  • Do not put your hand or a utensil into the unit.
  • Place a bucket under any leak and empty the cabinet.
  • Stop running water into the sink if the drain is backing up.
Symptoms

What the disposal is telling you

The location of the problem and the sound the unit makes narrow the cause quickly.

Humming, no spinning

A jam. A hex key in the center of the underside can free the flywheel in many units.

Nothing at all

Tripped reset button or breaker, or a failed motor.

Leak from the top

A loose or worn sink flange seal.

Leak from the bottom or side

A cracked housing, usually a sign the unit needs replacing.

Water backs up in the other side of the sink

The line past the disposal is clogged, not the disposal.

The line past the disposal

A clog that sits after the disposal looks like a disposal problem because water backs up into the sink. A plumber clears the line with a small cable, and checks that the dishwasher drain connection and air gap are not the cause. See our drain cleaning page.

Grease is the main enemy. Run cold water while the disposal runs, scrape pans into the trash and avoid coffee grounds, pasta, rice and fibrous scraps like celery. These habits keep the disposal and the pipe past it open.

Installing a new disposal

A replacement takes less than an hour for a typical kitchen. We remove the old unit, check the mounting, install the new one, connect the dishwasher drain and the power, and test for leaks. Horsepower matters. A larger motor handles tougher loads and runs quieter.

Some homes use a continuous feed model and some use a batch feed. We install the type you choose, and we make sure the electrical connection is safe. If the sink has a leak at the faucet, the faucet and sink repair page explains that side.

Code and permits note

Disposals connect to both the drain and a power supply, so two sets of standards apply. Plumbing connections follow the Illinois State Plumbing Code, and electrical work should be done by a qualified person.

Illinois plumbers are licensed through IDPH, which offers a license lookup, so you can check anyone you hire.
