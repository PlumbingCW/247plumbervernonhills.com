Where basement water comes from

Water gets into a basement in a few ways: through cracks in the wall or floor, at the joint where the floor meets the wall, through a window well, from a sump pit that is overwhelmed or from a sewer backup. Each has a different fix.

That is why a visit starts with questions. When does the water appear, where does it show up and how much? A leak that appears only after heavy rain points to groundwater, while a wet floor drain with a bad smell points to the sewer.

Warning signs

Signs of a basement water problem

Basement water leaves clues. These are the most common ones.

White chalky deposits on walls

Efflorescence, left behind when water moving through concrete evaporates.

Damp spots or stains on the floor

Seepage at the wall-floor joint or through cracks.

Musty smell

Moisture in the air, often with mold behind finishes.

Water at window wells

Clogged drains or a well that fills in heavy rain.

Water at the floor drain

Sewer or sump trouble. Check before treating it as seepage.

Waterproofing options

The least invasive option that solves the cause is usually best. Surface water fixes cost the least and are tried first.

The sump pump is part of the system

An interior drain system carries water to a sump pit, so the pump is critical. A reliable pump, a battery backup and a discharge that sends water outside are part of any plan. Our sump pump page explains the pump side.

Lake County Public Works bars sump and foundation drain water from the sanitary sewer. Any waterproofing plan discharges outdoors, away from the foundation. We make sure the design complies and explain how the water leaves the house.

What waterproofing cannot fix

Waterproofing does not stop a sewer backup, and it does not fix a leaking pipe. If water appears at the floor drain with a sewage smell, the problem is the sewer line. See our sewer line cleaning page.

A pipe leak inside a wall can also look like seepage. Leak detection helps rule out plumbing before waterproofing is considered. If you are unsure which problem you have, we say so and test before recommending work.

What it costs

What basement waterproofing costs

Waterproofing is priced by the method and the length of wall. We give a clear price before work starts after inspecting the basement and finding the water path.

Insurance rarely pays for waterproofing and often excludes seepage and groundwater. A backup endorsement may cover sudden events. Ask your insurer.

According to the village, building and plumbing rules both touch this work. Waterproofing sits between plumbing and building work, so permit needs vary by the scope.
