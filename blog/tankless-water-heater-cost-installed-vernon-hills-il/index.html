Tankless Water Heater Cost Installed: What Drives the Price in Vernon Hills

The price of a tankless water heater is more than the unit. Gas, venting, electrical work and water quality all change the final bill. This guide explains what to ask so quotes can be compared fairly.

Why tankless installs are priced differently

A tank heater is a tank, a gas or electric supply and a flue. A tankless unit heats water on demand and needs far more energy for a short time. That often means a larger gas line, a new vent made of special material, a condensate drain and sometimes a new electrical circuit.

Because every house is set up differently, any price you see online is a starting point. The accurate number comes from a plumber who looks at the existing gas line, venting path and wall space.

Tank or tankless: the honest comparison

Tankless units save space and never run out of hot water for a single use, and they can last longer than tanks. They cost more to install and need yearly maintenance. A tank is cheaper up front and simpler to replace.

If your gas line and venting already suit a tankless unit, the extra cost is lower. If the work involves opening walls, the price climbs. Our tankless water heater page explains when it makes sense, and our water heater replacement page covers a standard tank swap.

How to compare two quotes

Ask each contractor to put the same items in writing: unit model, gas line size, vent type and length, condensate drain, electrical work, permit, haul-away of the old tank and any water treatment. A quote that leaves any of these out may add them later as extras.

Ask about the warranty on the unit and the labor, and ask what maintenance is needed to keep the warranty valid. Ask whether the contractor is licensed, and check with IDPH if you want to verify it. Take time with the decision, because the installation is hard to change after the walls are closed.

We give a clear price before work starts, and we are glad to explain each line. If you would rather keep a tank, our water heater installation page explains that option.

Local note

Tankless and the village permit guide

The Village of Vernon Hills permit guide for water heaters lists the requirements for tank replacement, including an over-the-counter permit, a final inspection and a completion bond. The same guide describes tankless units as not needing a permit. That is helpful to know, but confirm the current rule at the building department, 847-367-3700, before you rely on it.

Water in the village comes from Lake Michigan through Lake County Public Works. The utility's report does not give a hardness number we would use, so test your tap before you decide how aggressively to treat scale.

Source type: Village of Vernon Hills permit guide and Lake County Public Works water quality report

When tankless is not the right call

Tankless is a poor fit when the gas line cannot be upgraded, when the venting path is blocked or when the household has several large simultaneous demands that exceed one unit's output. Two units or a hybrid system may solve it, at a higher cost.

It is also not ideal if the house has very hard water and no one will do the yearly flush. In those cases a tank or a heat pump heater may serve you better. We would rather tell you that before you buy than after the install.

Ongoing costs most buyers forget

Tankless heaters need to be flushed with a descaling solution on a schedule, more often with hard water. Skipping it shortens the life of the heat exchanger and may void the warranty. A service visit includes flushing, checking the inlet screen and testing combustion.

Our tankless water heater page describes the flush. Homes served from Mundelein and nearby towns should check their own water report, because hardness varies by supplier.

Rules and guidance note

The village permit guide says a tankless unit does not need a permit, but the guide is a few years old, so confirm with the Village of Vernon Hills building department at 847-367-3700. Gas and venting guidelines still apply.

Water quality matters too. Lake County Public Works publishes a report for the Lake Michigan supply.
