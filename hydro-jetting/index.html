When a repeat clog becomes a hydro jetting emergency

A single clog is a nuisance. A line that clogs again within weeks, or a main that backs up into a basement floor drain after a cable has been through it, is a pipe with something coating its walls. Hydro jetting is the tool built for that problem, and when sewage is reaching your floor it is also the fastest way to restore flow.

Callers often tell us the last plumber snaked the line and it stopped working again, or the cable got stuck partway. That pattern points to grease, scale or roots that a cable only punches through. Emergency hydro jetting in Vernon Hills starts with a call, a few questions about the pipe, and a plan for how to reach it.

While you wait

  • Stop using the washer, dishwasher and showers if the main line is backing up.
  • Keep people and pets out of any sewage water and open a window if the smell is strong.
  • Do not use chemical drain cleaners, because they do not reach the problem and make the line unsafe to open.
  • Locate your cleanout, usually a capped pipe near the foundation wall or in the basement floor, so the technician can reach it fast.

How hydro jetting works

A hydro jetter feeds a hose with a specially shaped nozzle through a cleanout into the pipe. The nozzle shoots high-pressure water jets in several directions. Rear-facing jets pull the hose forward, and the water scours the pipe wall, flushes debris downstream and cuts through grease and scale.

Residential machines commonly run at several thousand psi, with flow rates measured in gallons per minute. The pressure is adjusted to the pipe and the blockage. Technicians use different nozzles for grease and scale, for roots, and for a gentle rinse once the line is open. Our drain cleaning page covers cable and auger work for ordinary clogs.

Jetting or snaking: how we decide

Hydro jetting is not the answer to every clog. We recommend the lightest tool that will work, and we use a camera when we are not sure about the pipe's condition.

When we camera-check before jetting

High-pressure water is the wrong tool for a pipe that is cracked, collapsed or held together by habit. Orangeburg fiber pipe, badly deteriorated clay and cast iron with flaking scale can all be damaged or can release debris downstream. A short camera run shows what we are dealing with before pressure goes in.

If the footage shows a problem, we explain the options and the next step with you. That might be a spot fix on our sewer line repair page, or a full look using a sewer camera inspection before anything else is decided. We do not jet a pipe we think will fail.

Restaurants, shops and multi-unit buildings

Kitchen lines are the classic jetting job. A restaurant drain carries hot grease that cools and sticks, and a trap or interceptor that is overdue for pumping can push grease further down the line. Jetting between pumping dates keeps the building drain open.

Lake County Public Works, which serves the Vernon Hills area, has a sewer use ordinance that requires food service businesses to have a grease removal system. Owners must maintain it, which includes removing floating material at least every 90 days and pumping the unit fully on a set schedule. Jetting does not replace pumping, but it helps keep the line past the interceptor clear. See our commercial plumbing page for the after-hours service we offer businesses.

Roots, scale and what your pipe may be made of

Tree roots enter sewer lines at joints and small cracks, and they grow into dense mats that catch everything that passes. Mature trees and shrubs near a lateral are a common cause of repeat backups. Cutting the roots and then jetting is the standard sequence, and a camera afterwards shows what remains.

Scale builds in older iron and clay pipe from minerals and waste. The age and material of your pipe decide how aggressive we can be, and homes in Vernon Hills range from houses near the village's older core to newer subdivisions, so we confirm what is underground instead of assuming. If roots keep returning, a liner or a repair may be a better long-term answer than yearly jetting.
