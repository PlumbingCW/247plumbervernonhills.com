Where Gregg's Landing is

Gregg's Landing is a planned residential community in northeastern Vernon Hills, Illinois. A real-estate community profile puts it on about 813 acres with open space, small ponds and wetlands. It describes custom and semi-custom single-family homes, townhomes and condominiums.

The Village of Vernon Hills lists Gregg's Landing in its development records as a regional planned unit development on former Cuneo Estate land. One village ordinance describes that annexed land as north of Route 60, between Route 21 and Butterfield Road. A later resolution uses Butterfield Road, Milwaukee Avenue and the EJ&E railroad as location markers.

Those anchors place the community, but they are not a street-by-street boundary. We do not draw one here because the village pages we found do not give a written boundary for the whole community.

The sections inside the name

Real-estate sites list many sub-areas under the Gregg's Landing name. Single-family sections named include Augusta Green, Beaver Creek, Oakmont, Pine Valley, Saint Andrews, Royal Troon and The Pointe. Townhome and condo sections named include Bay Tree, Saddlebrook, Pinehurst and Shadow Creek. Muirfield Village appears in the same lists.

A 2014 sponsored article describes Augusta Green as 69 homes and Saddlebrook as 116 townhomes, both by Zale Homes. Names on listing sites do not always match the names on your closing papers, so your street is the safer detail to give us.

Plumbing questions that suit a community like this

The description of open space, ponds and wetlands says nothing about any single home's basement, so we do not claim that wet basements are common here. If you see water on a basement floor, our sump pump page covers the first checks.

Lawn irrigation lines are separate from the plumbing inside a house, but a leak in one can show up as a high water bill. Our leak detection page explains how a hidden leak is traced.

In the townhome and condo sections, an association usually sets rules about shutoff access and who repairs lines inside walls. Read your association documents before you call, because they decide who is responsible.
