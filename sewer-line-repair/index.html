When a sewer line problem is an emergency

Sewer pipe fails slowly and then all at once. A crack that lets roots in may cause a sluggish drain for months, and then a section collapses and nothing drains. If sewage is coming up a floor drain, a yard is sinking over the pipe, or a cable keeps hitting something solid, treat it as an emergency sewer line repair in Vernon Hills.

We start by confirming the problem with a camera. That tells us where the damage is, how long the damaged section runs, and what the pipe is made of. The right repair depends on all three.

Warning signs

Signs your sewer line is damaged

A damaged sewer line rarely announces itself with a single sign. These are the signals we most often hear about.

Repeat clogs at the same spot

A bellied, offset or root-filled section keeps catching debris. A cable stops at the same distance each time.

Sewage smell outside or in the basement

Gas or waste escaping through a crack. A smell near a cleanout or in the yard is a warning sign.

Soggy or sunken ground over the line

Water leaving a broken pipe softens the soil. A new low spot in a lawn or driveway can mean a collapse below.

Slow drains in the whole house

A main line that is partly blocked or deformed slows every fixture.

Sewer repair options compared

Trenchless methods such as lining and bursting can protect a yard, a driveway and landscaping. They do not fit every pipe, and a camera decides which method is realistic for yours.

Sewer line repair methods
MethodHow it worksBest fit
Spot repair (dig)Expose the bad section, cut it out and replace it with new pipe.A short damaged section that is easy to reach.
Pipe lining (cured in place)A resin-soaked liner is pulled through the pipe and cured to form a new pipe inside the old one.Cracked or root-prone pipe that is still round and continuous.
Pipe burstingA bursting head pulls new pipe through the old path, breaking the old pipe outward.Long runs of failed pipe where digging is a problem.
Full replacementOpen trench and new pipe along the whole run.Collapsed, offset or severely bellied pipe.

A short section with a root-filled joint may only need a spot repair, while a long run of cracked clay may be better lined. We explain the trade-offs honestly, including when digging is cheaper.

Cast iron and clay sewer pipe

Older homes often have cast iron building drains and clay sewer laterals. Cast iron rusts from the inside, flakes and eventually cracks or collapses. Clay is strong in compression but its joints open and invite roots.

A section of failing cast iron can often be repaired with a spot replacement using modern pipe and no-hub couplings. Clay with open joints is a good lining candidate if the pipe is round. Permits and inspections in Vernon Hills

The Village of Vernon Hills publishes a permit guide that lists sewer repair among permitted work and asks for a permit and a completion bond. The guide says the plumber can call the building department with the permit number to request an immediate inspection when the open trench is ready.

It also says plumbers should furnish a copy of their Illinois plumbing license and their contractor registration. The guide is a few years old, so we confirm today's requirements with the building department at 847-367-3700 before we dig. Inspections are weekdays only, which is why we plan the timing of an open trench with you.

Lake County Public Works sets the rules for connections to the public sanitary sewer, and its sewer use ordinance makes the owner responsible for the private building sewer. We mark and inspect before any tie-in to the public main.

Who pays for what: owner and utility

The property owner is responsible for the private building sewer, which usually runs from the house to the public main or cleanout. The utility maintains the public main. If the camera shows the problem is in the public main, we tell you and give you the footage so you can contact Lake County Public Works at 847-377-7500.

Your insurance may not cover sewer pipe damage from age, roots or settling. Some policies sell a sewer line endorsement, and some municipalities and utilities offer repair programs. Ask your insurer, and ask the village or county whether a program applies to you.
